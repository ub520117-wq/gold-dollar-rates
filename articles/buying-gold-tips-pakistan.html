Buying gold in Pakistan: 10 tips before you pay

Hallmarks, making charges, weighing tricks and the questions to ask the jeweller.

Buying gold is one of the largest cash purchases most Pakistani families ever make, and one of the least standardised. Two shops on the same street can quote different prices for the same piece. These ten tips will not make you an expert, but they will keep you from paying for someone else's.

1. Check the live rate before you leave home

Look up today's per-tola rate on this site before you visit the jeweller. You should know the metal value of what you are buying before anyone quotes you a price. If the shop's per-tola quote is far above the live rate, ask why — and be ready to walk out.

2. Ask for the karat in writing

22K is the standard for jewellery in Pakistan; 24K is pure gold, mostly sold as bars and coins. The price scales with purity, so a "cheap" piece may simply be lower karat. Get the karat written on the receipt.

3. Watch the weighing

Gold is weighed in front of you, on a scale you can see. Check that the scale reads zero before the piece goes on it, and that the weight on the receipt matches what you saw. A tenth of a tola is real money.

4. Separate the metal price from making charges

The bill should show the metal value and the making charges as separate lines. Making charges vary by design — plain work costs less than fine work — but they should be stated, not buried. Remember: making charges are not refunded when you sell.

5. Look for the hallmark

A hallmark stamp indicates the piece has been tested for purity. Its absence does not prove a piece is fake, but its presence is reassurance — and a jeweller who cannot explain the stamp on their own stock is a jeweller to avoid.

6. Buy from a shop you can find again

A jeweller with a long-standing shop, a name board and a reputation in the bazaar has something to lose. A temporary stall does not. The premium for buying from an established shop is the cheapest insurance in the trade.

7. Keep the receipt — forever

The receipt proves what you bought, at what weight and karat, and for how much. You need it to sell the piece back, to insure it, and to settle any dispute. Photograph it and keep a copy somewhere other than the jewellery box.

8. For investment, buy bars or coins — not jewellery

If the goal is investment rather than wearing, bars and coins carry the smallest premium over the metal value and the smallest loss on resale. Jewellery is for wearing; counting its making charges as an investment is the most expensive misunderstanding in the gold market.

9. Sell where you buy — or compare both

When selling, the shop that sold you the piece will usually offer the best buyback price, because it knows the piece. Still, compare with one other shop: buyback spreads vary, and the difference on a few tolas is worth an hour of your time.

10. Never rush a large purchase

Gold prices move daily, but they rarely move so fast that a day of thought costs you more than a hasty decision. If a jeweller pressures you to decide now, that is information — about the jeweller, not the market.
