How the gold rate in Pakistan is calculated

The exact formula: international spot price, the dollar rate, and the tola — with a worked example.

Every morning, jewellers across Pakistan put a new gold rate on their boards. The number looks local, but it is not decided locally. Pakistan's gold price is the international price of gold, converted into rupees, and expressed per tola. Once you understand the three inputs, you can work out the rate yourself — and you can tell at a glance whether a jeweller's quote is fair.

The formula in one line

24K gold per tola (PKR) = spot price per troy ounce (USD) × USD/PKR rate × 0.375

That is the whole calculation. Everything else on this page explains where each of those three numbers comes from.

Step 1: the international spot price

Gold is traded worldwide in US dollars per troy ounce. The "spot price" is the price for immediate delivery, set continuously on the world's gold exchanges — the London market is the most important benchmark. It moves all day, every weekday, in response to the same forces that move any market: central bank buying, investment demand, jewellery demand from India and China, interest rates, and fear. When the world is nervous, gold usually rises; when interest rates rise, gold often falls, because gold pays no interest and bonds start to look more attractive.

This site takes its spot price from a live market data feed, so the figure you see is the current market price, not a morning snapshot.

Step 2: the dollar-to-rupee rate

Because gold is priced in dollars, the rupee's value against the dollar is the second input — and for Pakistanis it is often the bigger one. Gold can fall in dollars and still rise in rupees, if the rupee falls faster. That is exactly what happened during several periods of rupee weakness: the international price was flat or falling, but the per-tola price in Pakistan kept climbing because each dollar cost more rupees. Two things move the local gold price, and the exchange rate is the one most buyers forget.

Step 3: the tola and purity

A troy ounce is 31.1035 grams. A tola is 11.6638 grams. Divide one by the other and you get 0.375 — one tola is 0.375 of a troy ounce. That is where the 0.375 in the formula comes from. For 22-karat gold, multiply the 24K result by 22/24 (0.9167); for 21-karat, multiply by 21/24 (0.875). Karat is simply the share of the metal that is gold: 24 out of 24 parts is pure, 22 out of 24 is 22K.

Worked example

Example with round numbers (not today's rates): suppose the spot price is $2,400 per ounce and the dollar is Rs 280. Per-tola 24K gold = 2,400 × 280 × 0.375 = Rs 252,000. The 22K price would be 252,000 × 22/24 = Rs 231,000. Change either input and the answer changes in proportion — a 1% rise in the dollar means a 1% rise in the local gold price, even if the world price never moves.

Why the shop price is higher

The formula gives the value of the metal. A jeweller's board rate adds a small premium for the local market, and the price of a finished piece adds making charges — the labour of turning gold into jewellery. Making charges vary by design and by shop, and they are not refunded when you sell the piece back. So the board rate and the rate on this page should be close, but the price of a finished necklace will always be higher than the metal value. If a jeweller's per-tola quote is far above the calculated rate, ask what is included — and walk away if the answer is vague.
