Tola vs gram: the units gold is weighed in

Why Pakistan quotes gold per tola, how to convert to grams and ounces, and the mistakes that cost buyers money.

On this page
  • What is a tola?
  • Converting between tola, gram and ounce
  • Why Pakistan still uses the tola
  • Mistakes that cost buyers money

Ask the price of gold in London and you will hear dollars per troy ounce. Ask in Karachi and you will hear rupees per tola. Both are the same gold, measured in different units — and the conversion between them is where buyers get confused and, sometimes, cheated.

What is a tola?

A tola is a traditional South Asian unit of weight, standardised at 11.6638 grams. It has been used for gold and silver across the subcontinent for centuries, long before the metric system arrived. One tola is also 0.375 of a troy ounce — the unit the world gold market uses — which is why the per-tola price is the per-ounce price multiplied by 0.375. Ten tolas make about 116.6 grams; a hundred grams is about 8.57 tolas.

Converting between tola, gram and ounce

1 tola = 11.6638 grams = 0.375 troy ounces
1 troy ounce = 31.1035 grams = 2.6667 tolas
1 gram = 0.0857 tolas

To convert a per-tola price to a per-gram price, divide by 11.6638. To convert per-gram to per-tola, multiply by 11.6638. The gold converter on this site does the arithmetic for you at today's live rate, for any weight in tolas, grams, 10 grams or troy ounces, and for 24K, 22K or 21K purity.

Why Pakistan still uses the tola

Habit and trust. Generations of buyers have thought in tolas: a wedding set is so many tolas, a gold biscuit is ten tolas, a family measures its savings in tolas. Jewellers quote in tolas because their customers think in tolas. The rest of the world moved to grams and ounces, but in the bazaar the tola is still the language of gold — and there is no sign of it changing. The important thing is not which unit you use, but that you can convert fluently, because the world price you are comparing against is quoted per ounce.

Mistakes that cost buyers money

The most common mistake is comparing prices in different units without converting — a per-gram price that looks cheap next to a per-tola price, for instance. The second is confusing the troy ounce with the ordinary (avoirdupois) ounce used for groceries: a troy ounce is 31.1035 grams, an ordinary ounce is 28.35 grams, and mixing them up understates the price by about 10%. The third is forgetting purity: a tola of 22K gold is worth 22/24 of a tola of 24K gold, so a quote that sounds cheap may simply be for lower-purity metal. Always ask three questions: how many tolas (or grams), what karat, and is the price for the metal only or including making charges. Get all three answers in writing on the receipt.
