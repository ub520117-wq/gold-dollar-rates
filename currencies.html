How currency rates are quoted

Every rate on this page is quoted against the US dollar first, because that is how the world's currency markets work — and then converted to rupees. The dollar rate is the anchor: when the dollar moves against the rupee, the euro, pound, dirham and riyal rates move with it, each in proportion to its own rate against the dollar. The dirham and riyal are special cases, because both are pegged to the dollar at fixed rates — so their rupee rates are simply the dollar rate divided by a constant. The euro and pound float freely, so their rupee rates have two stories in them: Europe's or Britain's story, and Pakistan's.
