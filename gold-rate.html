Jewellers in Pakistan add making charges, shop premiums and local market adjustments, so the price you are quoted in a shop will be higher — usually by a few thousand rupees per tola. Use these figures as a guide before you buy or sell, and confirm the final price with the jeweller.

Why the jeweller's price is higher

The rate on this page is the value of the metal itself. A jeweller adds making charges for the work of turning gold into jewellery, a shop premium, and sometimes a small margin for the day's price movement. Making charges vary by design — a plain bangle costs less to make than fine filigree work. When you sell gold back, the jeweller pays the metal value minus a small deduction, and making charges are not refunded. That is why jewellery is a poor short-term investment and a reasonable long-term store of value.

The other reason local prices can differ from this page is timing. The international spot price moves around the clock, while Pakistani shops typically set their board rate once or twice a day. On a day when gold jumps sharply, the board in the bazaar can lag the live price by hours.

Understanding purity: 24K, 22K, 21K

Karat measures how much of the metal is gold. 24 karat is pure gold — 99.9% gold, too soft for most jewellery on its own. 22 karat is 22 parts gold out of 24 (91.6% pure) and is the standard for jewellery in Pakistan and across South Asia: hard enough to wear every day, rich enough in colour to look like gold. 21 karat is 87.5% pure and is common in the Gulf. The price scales with purity, so a tola of 22K gold is worth 22/24 of a tola of 24K gold — the tables above already apply that maths.

When you buy, check the hallmark stamped on the piece and ask for the purity in writing on the receipt. A reputable jeweller states the karat and the weight on the bill without being asked twice.
