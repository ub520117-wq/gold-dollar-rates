About the Euro to rupee rate

The euro is the currency of 20 European Union countries and one of the world's most traded currencies. For Pakistan it matters in two ways: Europe is a major export market for Pakistani textiles, and a large Pakistani community lives and works in eurozone countries, sending remittances home. When the euro strengthens against the dollar, it usually strengthens against the rupee too — and vice versa.

Europe buys a large share of Pakistan's textile and garment exports, so exporters watch the euro rate as closely as the dollar rate. A stronger euro means each export order is worth more rupees, which is good news for exporters but pushes up the price of European imports such as machinery and medicines.

Remittances from the eurozone — particularly from Germany, France, Italy, Spain and the Netherlands — are a steady source of euros flowing into Pakistan. If you receive money from Europe, the euro-to-rupee rate decides how much lands in your family's hands, so it pays to compare transfer services: the rate and the fee together decide the winner.
